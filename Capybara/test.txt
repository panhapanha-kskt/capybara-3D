## Realism and 3D requirements
**Modeling**
- Rebuild or upgrade the flat parts into proper volumetric meshes: body, head, snout, ears, four paws and nose. Use clean quad topology with subdivision, and edge loops around the eyes, mouth, jaw, shoulders and wrists so they deform well.
- **Eyes:** separate eyeball spheres with cornea gloss, iris, pupil and a specular highlight. Add real **eyelids** that follow the eyeball rotation.
- **Mouth:** an actual mouth opening with a dark inner cavity, a small tongue and the capybara's two front teeth, so talking looks 3D.
- Keep the character readable when it is small: chunky shapes and clear eye and mouth contrast.

**Materials and fur**
- Use a Principled BSDF with **sheen** for the fur, **subsurface scattering** on the ears, nose and paw pads, a slightly moist glossy nose, and soft blush with a gradient in the cheek material.
- For the fur, use Geometry Nodes hair curves for rendered video. For the web GLB, fake it with a fur texture plus normal map, sheen and a fresnel rim. Real hair curves don't export to glTF.
- Bake textures (base color, normal, roughness, AO) so the GLB matches the render.

**Rig**
- Use a proper armature: root, hips, spine (2–3 bones), chest, neck, head, jaw, two ears (2 bones each, for bending), arms with **IK** for the paws, legs/feet and an eye **look-at target**.
- Add **secondary motion** on the ears, cheeks and belly: jiggle or spring behavior, then **bake it into keyframes** so it exports.
- Use shape keys for the facial expressions: blink L/R, happy eyes, brows up/down/knit, the visemes, cheek puff and blush intensity, plus corrective shape keys where joints deform badly.
- Keep it web-safe: at most 4 bone influences per vertex, and a sensible bone count of about 30–50.

**Motion quality (make it feel alive and weighty)**
- Apply the 12 animation principles: anticipation, squash and stretch on the body, follow-through and overlapping action (ears, cheeks, belly), arcs, ease in/out and offset timing between body parts.
- Breathing moves the chest and belly first, then the shoulders and head lightly.
- Eyes: saccades, blinks every 3–6 seconds at irregular intervals with a slight eyelid squash, and the head leads its turns with the eyes.
- Add subtle continuous micro-motion (a noise layer) in every state, so it never looks frozen.
- Keep weight believable: the body settles after every move instead of stopping dead.

**Lighting and camera (for renders and previews)**
- Use a soft studio setup: a warm key light, a cool fill, a rim light to separate the fur from the background, a soft HDRI, and ambient occlusion with contact shadows under the body.
- Use a close-up chest-up framing suited to a chat widget, a slight depth of field, and motion blur on renders.
